# Authorship and contributions

Last updated: 2026-11-18.

## Credit by role

Contributors are credited by the role they played.
The names of the roles are the terms of CRediT (Contributor Roles Taxonomy), used as they are.
Who played which role is written in the issue of the collaboration, and in what is published.

## Authors

An author is a person who meets the authorship criteria of the venue and agrees to publication.
A contribution that does not meet the criteria is credited in the acknowledgements and in the list of roles.
The order of authors is decided by all authors in the issue of the collaboration before publication.

## Claude is not an author

Claude is not an author.
Claude is not in the list of roles either.
The same holds for any other language model.

## Disclosure of language-model use

The operator works with Claude Code.
Everything published states what a language model was used for.
When a collaborator uses a language model, the issue of the collaboration asks for a note of what it was used for.
The disclosure is never removed.

## How names are shown

A name is shown in the form its holder chooses.
It may be a real name or a GitHub account name.
The operator does not ask for a real name.
The way a name is shown can be changed later through [Requests](./requests.en.md).
The reading of the law is under "Copyright Act" in [Reading of the law](./law.en.md).

## Responsibility

Each author is responsible for the part that author took on.
When an error in a proof or a number is found, the published draft is not rewritten, and the correction goes into the errata.
